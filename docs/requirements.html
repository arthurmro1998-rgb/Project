1 Overview

The platform lets a customer pay to have a contractor reach out to a specified person on Instagram, exchange messages on the customer's behalf, and hand back proof of that exchange as screenshots. The customer creates an account, places and pays for an order, and later logs in to view the delivered screenshots — visible only to them.

This document lists what the system needs to do, organized by area. Each requirement has an ID for tracking and a priority: Must blocks launch, Should matters soon after, Could is a nice-to-have.

2 User Roles

Customer

Signs up, pays for a service, and views the delivered screenshots for their own orders only.

Contractor

Assigned an order, performs the Instagram outreach, and uploads screenshots as the deliverable.

Admin

Assigns orders to contractors, handles disputes/refunds, and can access any account or deliverable for support.

Screenshots are the product the customer paid for. They need to be private by default — visible to the owning customer and admins, invisible to everyone else, including other customers and contractors not assigned to that order.

8 Data Model

You don't need to know database internals to know what needs to be tracked. Roughly, five kinds of records:

  • Users — email, hashed password, role (customer/contractor/admin), verification status.
  • Orders — which customer, target Instagram account, instructions, status, assigned contractor, timestamps.
  • Payments — Stripe payment/session ID, order it belongs to, amount, status. No card numbers ever live here.
  • Deliverables — pointer/reference to each screenshot file in storage, which order it belongs to, upload timestamp.
  • Access log — who viewed which deliverable, when (supports SEC-05).
In practice: a managed relational database (e.g. Postgres) for Users/Orders/Payments/Access-log, plus a private object storage bucket for the actual image files, with the database holding just a reference to each file. This is the standard split — small structured records in the database, large binary files in storage built for them.

10 Risks & Open Questions

Instagram's terms of use. Instagram's platform policy restricts automated messaging and paid outreach services conducted through third-party accounts. Since contractors are messaging targets on a customer's behalf, there's real risk of account bans (and possibly bigger platform/legal exposure) regardless of whether the sending is manual or automated. Worth a short conversation with a lawyer before this goes live, and worth deciding now whether contractors use their own accounts or customer-provided accounts.
PIPEDA (Canadian privacy law). As a Canada-based business, PIPEDA governs how personal information is collected, used, and disclosed — and it applies not just to customer accounts/payment data, but to information about the Instagram targets being messaged, since that's personal information about a person flowing through the platform too. Expect to need: a privacy policy stating what's collected and why, meaningful consent from customers, a process for someone to request access to or deletion of their data, and a breach-notification plan. This compounds with the Instagram ToS risk above — worth covering both with a lawyer in the same conversation. Note: not selling the data or using it for marketing doesn't remove these obligations, but it does narrow the "purpose" you have to disclose and simplifies the consent language, since you're not seeking consent for secondary uses.
No guarantee of outcome. The Terms of Service should be explicit that the contractor can attempt outreach but cannot guarantee a response from the target, and that the deliverable is the message screenshots themselves, not any particular outcome.
Contractor vetting. Not yet defined — decide whether contractors apply, get manually approved by an admin, and/or sign an independent-contractor agreement before receiving orders.
